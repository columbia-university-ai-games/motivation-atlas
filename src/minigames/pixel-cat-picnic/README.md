# Pixel Cat Picnic

Motivator: Chance. Author: Alicey66.

## Mechanic

Click or tap fish; use Tab and Enter for keyboard play, and arrows to move. Each level has five hearts, a 90-second limit and an eight-second hunger timer. Targets are 500, 800 and 1,200 points. One level per mode suffices for comparison; continuing is optional. Randomness ON hides 0–30-point rewards and randomizes positions and effects. OFF uses fixed locations, repeatable events and 15-point fish. Both modes average 15 points per fish.

## Dynamic

One player tried ON, then OFF. With ON, they were more willing to open mystery boxes and replay after clearing a level. Bad-item clicks prompted stronger reactions. With OFF, they did not want to click pickups because outcomes were predictable. Uncertainty appeared to encourage optional interactions. This comparison concerns overall randomness, not reward variance alone. One player and a fixed testing order limit the conclusion. Counterbalanced testing could help.

## Aesthetic

The player found ON more fun and appeared noticeably more excited. OFF made time feel long; predictable outcomes removed anticipation. They felt fifteen points per bite was too little and lacked impact, despite the same expected points per fish. Perceived reward seemed to matter more than its average. Their preference suggests anticipation mattered alongside the points themselves.
