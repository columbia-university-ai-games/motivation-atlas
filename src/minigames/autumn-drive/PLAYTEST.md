# Autumn Drive playtest

## Before the session

Open the Atlas Submission page at `http://127.0.0.1:5173/#/m/submission`
while `npm run dev` is running. Scroll to **Autumn Drive: a different pace**.
Start once to load the original 3D assets. A keyboard is the easiest first try;
the pad also supports holding and sliding with a mouse or a finger.

Oscar can first check that the controls feel right. The assignment's evidence
comes from **another person** playing the two conditions. Give them a brief,
unrecorded practice with the controls, then use the same device and route for
both recorded runs. Call them Player A; do not collect identifying details.

## Two recorded runs

1. Check **30-second deadline** and press **Restart drive**. Tell the player:
   "Follow the road through the three gold gates. You can press R or Recover
   to road if you get stuck." Observe without coaching their driving choices.
2. After the run, ask: "What were you trying to do? How did that feel?"
   Record an action you actually saw and their own words. Expand **Run notes
   for your playtest** for the result, elapsed time, gates and recoveries.
3. Uncheck the deadline and press **Restart drive**. The checkbox applies on
   starting, not during the previous run. Repeat the same route and questions.
4. Ask: "What changed between the two? What made you feel that way?"
   Do not suggest that one mode ought to be relaxing or stressful. The
   untimed run can continue past 30 seconds; use **End this run** if the player
   chooses to stop, and record it as stopped rather than completed.
5. Copy or dictate the run notes before leaving this page; they are held only
   in memory. Keep practice runs separate from the two recorded conditions.

The on-then-off order can create a learning effect: the player already knows
the road on the second run. Mention that limit when interpreting the change.
Automated driving checks are software validation, not a human playtest.

## Dictation for the final README

"With the deadline on, Player A [did this], finished/stopped at [result], and
said [their words]. With it off, they [did this] and said [their words]. The
unexpected part was [observation]. I think the rule affected play by [reason],
although [limit or alternative explanation]. One Codex suggestion I kept,
changed or rejected was [suggestion], because [my reason]."

Use that evidence to finish the roughly 250-word README: **Mechanic** explains
the rule and controls; **Dynamic** describes observed behaviour; **Aesthetic**
reports the player's feelings; **Sources** contains the exact course-note
quotation, citation and credits. Then finalize the PR, check CI, and
submit its URL to CourseWorks.
