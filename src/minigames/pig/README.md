# Pig: push your luck

Motivator: Chance. Author: the course.

## Mechanic

Roll a die. A 1 ends your turn and loses everything you rolled that turn; any other face adds to your turn total; hold to bank it. First to 50 wins. Two toggles change the rules:

- **Push your luck** on or off. Off means one roll per turn, banked at once.
- **Reward:** the die's face, or a steady 4 for every safe roll. Both pay the same on average.

## Dynamic

With push your luck on, every roll is a decision under risk, and the lead swings back and forth. Turn it off and the decision disappears: the game plays itself. Switch to the steady reward and the swings shrink while the average stays the same.

## Aesthetic

Tension and relief: the hope before each roll, the groan at a 1, the gamble on one more. The bot holds at 20, the rule that maximizes expected points per turn (Neller and Presser 2004, "Optimal Play of the Dice Game Pig," *The UMAP Journal* 25 (1)); it never feels any of this.

## Sources

From the Chance section of the course note:

- Pushing your luck, as in the dice game Pig, "astonishingly simple, strategically deep, and increasingly dramatic" (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 15, "Pig").
- Reversals of fortune, as the chutes and ladders of Chutes and Ladders produce (Salen and Zimmerman 2003, ch. 15, "Chance and Game Play").
- Chance alternating with skill, "an alternating pattern of tension and relaxation" (Schell 2019, p. 225).
- Variable rewards: a one-in-three chance of thirty points "stays rewarding for a much longer time" than ten points every time, though the average is the same (Schell 2019, p. 233).
